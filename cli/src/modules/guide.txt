#[nowallet] You do not have a wallet open yet. Type `wallet create` to make one — a name and a
password — or `open` if you have made one before. Then come back to `guide`.

WHAT YOU ARE HOLDING. Marigold money comes as notes of fixed sizes — 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10 and up —
that you hold and hand over, like cash. A note has no owner on record anywhere: whoever holds its
key holds the money. This wallet keeps those keys in its vault.

YOUR FIRST NOTES. On the test network, faucet.marigold.cash gives out real notes: paste each one
into `receive`, biggest first. Otherwise someone pays you: `request` prints a code to
show them (add an amount, `request 5`, to ask for exactly that), and the wallet waits for
the payment to land. Handed a code instead? `receive <code>` takes the money.

#[ledger] An exchange pays to an address instead. That is what your ledger is for: `address`
shows it, and whatever arrives there is turned into notes by the wallet on its own, usually
within a minute.

#[notesonly] This wallet keeps notes only — it has no ledger, so nothing can be paid to it by
address. If an exchange ever needs to pay you, `account create bip32` adds a ledger; the same 24
words bring it back.

PAYING. `pay 20` makes a code worth 20 — show it or send it, and `receive` on their side
takes it; the wallet adds a 0.01 so they can make it theirs, and picks the fewest notes
that cover it. Shown a request code instead? `pay <code>` pays it with nothing to hand
over. Paying someone who only gave you an address, such as an exchange?
`exchange <address> <amount>` sends it straight from your notes in one go. A code you
hand over is cash: anyone who sees it can take it, so give it straight to the receiver.

#[notesonly] Without a ledger there is nowhere to put change, so `exchange` needs notes that make
up the amount to within 0.01 — pay 1.10, not 1.07, if you hold a 1 and a 0.1. The wallet tells
you when they do not fit.

CHECKING. `balance` is the whole answer: your notes, by size. The prompt shows the same figure.
`history` lists what you have paid and received; `note list` lists every note you hold.

KEEPING IT SAFE. Your password and a backup bring your money back — nothing else can, and there
is no one to ask. `backup <file>` puts the whole wallet into one file under a passphrase of your
choosing; make one once you hold anything, and again after anything big arrives. That file alone
rebuilds the wallet anywhere with `wallet restore <file>`. Anyone holding the file and its
passphrase can spend your money — treat it as cash. `otp on` makes the wallet ask for a code from
your phone before it spends. Prefer paper? `note vault words` prints 24 recovery words that,
together with a copy of the vault files, bring the wallet back without the password.

#[ledger] IF YOU MINE. `mine start` mines with spare CPU on your own node. Rewards land on your
ledger and the wallet turns them into notes by itself; `balance` shows the ledger line while it
does. If it ever says there is too much to tidy on its own, `sweep <amount>` does part of it at
a time.

YOUR OWN NODE. The wallet can run a Marigold node on this machine, so nobody else sees which
notes you ask about. `connect` offers it; the first sync takes a while and the wallet says so.
Until then it can use a public node if you say yes — it never does that unasked.

EVERYTHING ELSE. `help` lists the everyday commands. `advanced on` shows the technical side —
every command, addresses, and the reason behind an error — and `advanced off` hides it again.
